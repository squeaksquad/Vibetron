Design review · AU / VST3

Vibetron VT-369

Here are three hardware directions for the plugin, shown as live mockups. The needles run on VU ballistics driven by simulated programme material. Switch modes on any unit to see its theme. Each mode reskins the whole faceplate and loads its own liner notes.

MODE ISilkair · openness
MODE IIVelvetwarmth · density
MODE IIIObsidiandepth · holography

Direction AWalnut & Brass

1970s studio outboard: oiled walnut cheeks, a brushed faceplate, backlit paper meters and a chicken-head selector. The faceplate lacquer changes per mode.

Feel: warm, familiar, premium. Reads well at small sizes.
Mode cue: champagne → oxblood → black lacquer with gold legends. A jewel pilot lamp and louvres add hardware texture.

Direction BBroadcast Console

A 1960s transmission-desk module: hammertone enamel, rack ears, illuminated push-buttons, and the mode notes on a taped index card.

Feel: engineered, characterful, a little playful.
Mode cue: machine grey → BBC green → graphite navy, with a different lamp colour for each.
Tradeoff: the busiest texture of the three, and the loudest in a crowded session.

Direction CMidnight Glass

A flagship hi-fi amplifier after dark: a black glass fascia, gold legends, and meters glowing through smoked glass.

Feel: luxurious, cinematic, most modern of the three.
Mode cue: meter glow shifts warm gold → rose → sapphire.
Tradeoff: relies on glow, so it needs a dark DAW theme to shine.
Built: this is the direction implemented in the plugin, including the machined knob.
Meters are calibrated to 0 VU = −18 dBFS (switchable to −14 or −20), with 300 ms integration and about 1.5% overshoot. Surfaces use brushed metal, walnut, paper and film grain throughout.
